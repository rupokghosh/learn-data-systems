Reliability: when things go wrong

A reliable system keeps doing what users expect, even when parts of it break. Parts will break, so the goal is not zero faults. It is making sure faults do not reach the user.

A fault is not a failure

A faultfault: One component deviating from its spec: a dead disk, a crashed process, a slow network link. is one part of the system misbehaving. A failurefailure: The system as a whole stops providing the service users expect. is when the whole system stops working for its users. A fault-tolerant system stops faults from turning into failures.

You cannot prevent every fault, so good teams practise on them. Some even crash their own servers on purpose, at random, to prove the system copes.

Hardware faults: rare per machine, constant at scale

Rare events become routine at scale. The answer is redundancy: spare disks, duplicate power supplies, and copies of the data on several machines, so one dead part is a non-event.

Software faults: fewer, but they strike everywhere at once

Hardware faults are mostly independent: one disk dying does not make its neighbour more likely to die. Software bugs are different. The same bug runs on every machine, so one bad input or one odd date can take them all down together. A slow service can also make its callers retry, which piles on more load, which makes it slower: a cascading failure.

Human faults: the biggest source of outages

People design, configure, and operate these systems, and people make mistakes. Configuration changes are behind a large share of real outages. You cannot train mistakes away, so make them cheap:

  • Design APIs and tools that make the right thing easy and the wrong thing hard.
  • Give people a realistic sandbox to experiment in, away from real users.
  • Make rollback fast, and roll out changes to a few machines first.
  • Monitor everything, so you notice problems early.

Reliability is not only a concern for banks and aircraft. A shop that goes down loses sales, and a photo app that loses someone's family pictures loses their trust forever. Cutting corners on reliability should be a conscious decision, not an accident.
